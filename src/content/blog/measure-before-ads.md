When enquiries are slow, increasing ad spend feels like the most direct response. It can work — but only if you can tell what is happening after someone clicks. Without that, more budget simply buys more uncertainty.

## Agree on the action that matters

Before looking at platforms or audiences, define the action that counts as a useful result. A newsletter signup, a quote request, a phone call and a completed booking are not interchangeable. Pick the one that best represents progress for the business, and make sure everyone agrees on it.

Secondary actions can still be tracked, but they should not be allowed to obscure the main one. A campaign that generates plenty of clicks and no qualified conversations is not a success just because the click numbers look healthy.

## Check the basics first

A simple measurement audit usually covers:

- **Form submissions:** Are they recorded, and does the team receive them reliably?
- **Phone calls:** Can you tell which calls came from a campaign?
- **CRM stages:** Do leads have a clear status after the first conversation?
- **Source data:** Is the original source preserved when a lead is entered manually?
- **Reporting:** Can you compare periods without rebuilding a spreadsheet?

If any of these are missing, fix them before increasing spend. Otherwise the new data will be incomplete in the same way as the old data.

## Separate the platform from the business

Ad platforms report on what happened inside their own environment. Your CRM reports on what happened in the business. Both are useful, but they answer different questions. A campaign may look efficient in-platform while producing leads that never qualify. Or it may look expensive while producing a small number of high-value conversations.

Connecting the two views is what makes the reporting useful. At minimum, every lead should carry its source into the CRM, and every stage change should be visible over time.

## Give the test a fair window

Short tests tend to reward noise. Decide in advance how long a test will run and what result would change your mind. Avoid changing the creative, audience and landing page at the same time; if everything changes, nothing can be learned.

Keep notes on what was tested and what happened. A simple log is more valuable than a complicated dashboard that nobody opens.

## Spend with a reason

More budget is a reasonable decision when the path from click to customer is visible and the unit economics make sense. It is a poor decision when it is being used to compensate for unclear tracking or a slow follow-up process.

Measure first. Then spend with a specific question in mind. That is a slower start, but it usually leads to better decisions.
